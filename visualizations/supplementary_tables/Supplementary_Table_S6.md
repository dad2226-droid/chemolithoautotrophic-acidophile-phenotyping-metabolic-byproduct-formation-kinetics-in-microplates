# Supplementary Table S6. Ferric–ferrous separation at 380 and 428 nm

The ferric–ferrous contrast in AU is larger at 380 nm than at 428 nm across the same supported concentrations. The wavelength-difference row retains within-plate wavelength pairing.

**Figure panels** 1e · [All Supplementary tables](README.md)

**Column definitions**

- `readout` — Absorbance at 380 nm (A380) or 428 nm (A428), or their within-plate difference A380 − A428.
- `ferric_plate_n` — Number of physical microplates contributing a mean response for ferric standards.
- `ferrous_plate_n` — Number of physical microplates contributing a mean response for ferrous standards.
- `common_level_n` — Number of measured ferric reference concentrations with a finite response on every plate at both wavelengths after alignment of the ferrous series by interpolation.
- `reference_min_M` — Lowest measured total Fe concentration on the common reference grid, in M.
- `reference_max_M` — Highest measured total Fe concentration on the common reference grid, in M.
- `estimate` — Mean ferric minus mean ferrous response (AU), giving equal weight to common concentrations within a plate and to plates within a chemical series.
- `ci_low` — Lower 95% Welch t confidence limit for the ferric-minus-ferrous contrast, in AU.
- `ci_high` — Upper 95% Welch t confidence limit for the ferric-minus-ferrous contrast, in AU.
- `welch_df` — Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom for the confidence interval, calculated from the variance among plates and the plate count in each chemical series.
